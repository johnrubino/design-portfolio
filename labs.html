Where Did I Leave Off?

Navori is a lightweight context-tracking tool I built to solve a recurring problem: losing my train of thought across multiple long-running projects.

Dimension Details
Type Personal Side Project
Role Solo — Design & Development
Timeline 6 weeks (design to launch)
Status Shipped — active daily use

Product overview — Navori home view

⚡

Context Evaporates

I work across multiple long-running projects simultaneously — client work, side builds, writing. When I return to a project after days away, I spend a surprising amount of time reconstructing where I was: which decisions were in-flight, what I was about to try, what dead ends I'd already ruled out.

Notes apps were too freeform. Task managers tracked what to do, not what state I was in. The closest substitute was a sticky note on my monitor — which doesn't scale, doesn't persist, and doesn't travel.

"The problem isn't remembering what to do next. It's remembering what you were thinking when you left."

Problem space mapping — the gap between task management, notes, and context state

⚙️

A Checkpoint, Not a Todo List

The core model is a "checkpoint" — a short-form state capture you fill in before closing out a project session. Three fields: what was I doing, what's the immediate next decision, any blockers.

When you return, you don't scan Notion or reconstruct Slack threads — you read your own last checkpoint. Thirty seconds to write, thirty seconds to read. The overhead needed to be low enough that it would actually get used.

Core design constraint
If saving a checkpoint takes longer than reconstructing context organically, the tool fails. Friction is the only real enemy of a habit-forming utility.

Checkpoint form — three-field capture pattern

✨

Calm by Default

The interface needed to feel minimal and fast — not another dashboard to manage. The home view shows projects sorted by last activity, with each card displaying the most recent checkpoint inline. No drilling required to see where you are.

Adding a checkpoint is a modal with three short fields. Auto-saves as you type. Keyboard-first throughout — the entire flow runs without touching a mouse. Opening a project, writing a checkpoint, and closing out in under 20 seconds was the target.

🔮

What Building It Taught Me

This was the first product I shipped end-to-end solo — design, frontend, backend, deployment. The technical ceiling on design decisions was lower than I expected, which forced a useful discipline: if I couldn't build it in a day, it probably wasn't the right idea.

The most interesting design problem ended up being the checkpoint form itself. Early versions had five fields — too many. Three is the right number. Two feels incomplete; four becomes a form. Three is a ritual.

I use Navori every day. That's the only metric that matters for something built to solve your own problem.
